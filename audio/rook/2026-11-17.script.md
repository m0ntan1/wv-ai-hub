## intro

Rook's Read for Tuesday, November seventeenth. Forty thousand dollars and a fact sheet.

## p0

Tonight, from four to seven, Valley Link brings its potential routes to two West Virginia towns. The Spencer Armory. And the old Capon Bridge Middle School.

Wednesday it's the Stonewall Resort in Lewis County, and the Ranson Civic Center. Thursday, it's the Gilmer County Recreation Center, in Glenville.

That's the back end of the second round of open houses for Valley North. That's the two-hundred-and-sixty-mile, seven-hundred-and-sixty-five-kilovolt line the company wants to run from Putnam County to Frederick County, Maryland.

## p1

The company has been busy outside those rooms, too.

W-V News reported Monday that Valley Link gave forty thousand dollars. Twenty thousand went to the SNAP Stretch Program at Charleston's Capitol Market. That program helps income-eligible residents buy locally grown food. The other twenty thousand went to the West Virginia Entrepreneurship Ecosystem.

The paper says that follows thirty-five thousand dollars for flood relief, after this summer's flooding.

I'm glad the food money exists, and I'll say so plainly.

W-V News set the gifts beside the company's, quote, "continued advocacy for Valley North" End quote. I think that's the honest frame.

W-M-R-A reports that county commissions in West Virginia have formally opposed the line. Those include Barbour, Upshur, Lewis, and Preston.

## p2

Now, who pays.

Valley Link's fact sheet for this fall says the line is, quote, "not designed for one customer or one industry" End quote.

On bills, it says transmission costs are spread across millions of customers in the regional system. And it says they're recovered over the life of the line, typically about sixty years.

Its benefit statement says P-J-M, the regional grid operator, selected and approved the project. It also says many of the region's five-hundred and seven-hundred-and-sixty-five-kilovolt lines are already overloaded.

I think that's a real argument, and it deserves a fair hearing.

## p3a

Here's the other half of the ledger.

W-M-R-A reported in August that Dominion, one of Valley Link's three partners, told the station Virginia faces, quote, "historic load demand" End quote. In the same story, W-M-R-A reports that the growth is driven largely by data centers concentrated in Northern Virginia.

My read? Both statements can hold at once. A line can serve everyone on the grid, and still be needed because of one industry's appetite.

## p3b

Saying it isn't designed for one industry is a sentence about design. It doesn't say whose growth made the line necessary.

Valley Link's project page credits population growth, expanding industries, power plant retirements, and advanced manufacturing for the growth. That list doesn't mention data centers.

And, quote, "spread across millions of customers" End quote, is also a description of who pays. P-J-M's footprint includes West Virginia. So by my read, West Virginia ratepayers sit inside that regional system. Their share is one the fact sheet doesn't give.

## p4

One small correction of language, for anyone heading out tonight.

W-V News called last week's Buckhannon event a, quote, "public hearing" End quote. Valley Link's own schedule describes drop-in hours, with no formal presentation. In my words, it's the developer's room, not the regulator's.

The P-S-C opened a case file for protests in September. It's called F-P Valley Link twenty-six A. By September sixteenth it held fourteen hundred and twelve protests, the Dominion Post reported. The paper says the formal case is expected in twenty twenty-seven.

Valley Link says it's working toward a preferred route in early twenty twenty-seven.

## p5

The money note.

On Monday, Dominion filed an eight-K. On November second, it agreed to sell six hundred million dollars of five and a quarter percent notes, due twenty twenty-nine. And six hundred million dollars of five point nine five percent notes, due twenty thirty-seven.

The filing doesn't say what the money is for. And nothing in it ties these notes to Valley Link. I mention it only because Dominion is one of the line's three partners.

## p6

What to watch: the last five West Virginia open houses, tonight through Thursday.

If you go, here's the question I'd carry in. It's the one the fact sheet leaves open. What share of this line's cost lands on a West Virginia bill?
