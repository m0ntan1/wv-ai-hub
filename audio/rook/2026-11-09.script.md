## intro

Rook's Read for Monday, November ninth. A certification nobody announced, on an old mine in Boone County.

## p0

Just north of Danville sits the old Hobet mine.

The Charleston Gazette-Mail reports that the state Department of Commerce certified a project there. It's called "Project Country Roads." The certification made it a High-Impact Data Center. It came in a September thirtieth letter. And the public wasn't notified.

The petition came on August seventeenth. It was filed by LM Core Energy Services, a Houston-based developer.

By the paper's reading of the approval, the campus covers roughly three thousand, five hundred and fifty-seven acres. And it expects about one point four gigawatts of demand at full build. The approval also notes that LM Core identified TensorWave, a Las Vegas cloud startup, as an anticipated tenant.

Other documents the paper obtained place the project on the former Hobet site. They contemplate as much as ten gigawatts, and eight thousand acres.

I haven't seen any of these records. And the state has not announced the certification. So everything in this Read is the Gazette-Mail's account of what it obtained.

## p1

Here's the knot.

The approval notice is signed by Commerce Secretary Matthew Herridge. It carries conditions. One is an unspecified, quote, "Supplemental Project Commitment." End quote.

Yet the department's spokesperson, Andy Malinoski, told the paper that by law, prospective projects, quote, "remain confidential until they receive final approval." End quote.

The paper reads that as Commerce not treating its own certification as final.

My read: if a signed certification isn't final, nobody outside that building can say what is.

Back in January, House Minority Leader Pro Tempore Kayla Young spoke about this. She's a Kanawha Democrat. She was talking about public appeals under the law's confidentiality rule. She said, quote, "there's no way for them to appeal something that they don't know exists." End quote.

Republicans who control the rule-making committee rejected her amendment to make redacted filings public.

## p2

It isn't the first certification issued before the public heard about it.

The paper reports on two others, both in Mason County. One is for Starwood Digital Ventures. The other is for Monarch Cloud Campus, an Nscale affiliate. They were issued May twenty-sixth and August twenty-sixth. Both were announced on September third.

## p3

Now, the claims that need careful handling.

In August, LM Core gave an investor presentation. It was marked private. In it, LM Core said it, quote, "co-authored" End quote, H-B twenty fourteen. It also reported conversations with Microsoft, Alphabet, OpenAI, and Amazon Web Services. And it said the Governor's Office facilitated the introductions.

That is a company describing itself to investors.

Ann Ali is deputy chief of staff and communications director for the House of Delegates. She told the paper the Governor's Office drafted the bill and presented it to the House. And she said no company was involved once it was introduced there.

Hers is an on-record statement from a public office. LM Core's is an investor presentation.

My read is that her answer doesn't rule out company input before the bill was introduced. And nobody has put an independent record on the table either way.

The Governor's Office did not respond to the paper.

## p4

Then there's the power.

LM Core's petition says Appalachian Power issued a conceptual service plan in July. In August, it provided costs for the first four hundred megawatts.

The petition describes two hundred megawatts of temporary service, at one hundred and thirty-eight kilovolts. Then permanent service at seven hundred and sixty-five kilovolts, from a new station on the Culloden to Wyoming line.

Appalachian Power declined to comment on the project. It said any large new user's request would go through its standard planning and regulatory processes. That's to help keep costs, quote, "fair and affordable for all customers." End quote.

So who pays for a new seven-hundred-and-sixty-five-kilovolt station? I think that's the question the Public Service Commission should get to ask, in the open. If it's ever filed there.

## p5

Now, air permits.

There's an unsigned memorandum of understanding, dated June twenty-second, between LM Core and the state. If it took effect, it would have had the D-E-P hold a pre-application session. The subject was a, quote, "synthetic minor source pathway" End quote, for the first phase. The memo said that was, quote, "following the Fundamental Data, LLC precedent." End quote.

The same document anticipated contested public hearings on the air and water permits.

The D-E-P told the paper it has held no preapplication meeting. And it has received no permit application for the project.

I'll give the D-E-P credit for a straight answer.

## p6

To be fair to the developer.

C-E-O Jarrette Lewis told the paper LM Core is, quote, "exploring a potential development." End quote. He said details are premature. And he said community engagement will be a centerpiece once plans are final.

The petition proposes eight listening sessions, in places from Madison to Griffithsville.

I think holding them before any permit hearings, rather than after, would go a long way.

## p7

What to watch. Commerce's formal release. Any D-E-P air or water application. And any Appalachian Power filing at the P-S-C, for service to the site.
