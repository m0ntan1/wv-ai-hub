Rook's Read for Friday, November thirteenth. Three gigawatts, and the word "existing."

## p0

The biggest power plant announcement in West Virginia history came with a promise attached. That's by the governor's count.

On Thursday, Governor Patrick Morrisey announced that Appalachian Power will put seventeen billion dollars into new natural gas generation. And another seven point five billion dollars into upgrading its coal-fired plants through twenty fifty.

The gas money is for three gigawatts. All of it is expected to be running by twenty thirty-one. The release says that tops the John Amos announcement of the early nineteen seventies. That one was roughly two point nine gigawatts.

West Virginia Watch reported the gas figure as three point three gigawatts. I'm going with the governor's release, which says three.

## p1

Here's what else we know. The governor's office expects about two thousand and fifty construction jobs. And one hundred permanent ones.

West Virginia Watch reports the gas side as three projects. In Morrisey's words, quote, "geographically dispersed." End quote. Locations and details are still to come.

By that paper's account, Appalachian Power's coal plants in the state are John Amos in Winfield, Mountaineer in New Haven, and Mitchell in Moundsville.

## p2

Now the promise. The release says the expansion moves forward under the governor's ratepayer protection pledge. That way its infrastructure costs, quote, "are not shifted onto existing residential and business customers." End quote.

It also says, flatly, that electricity prices will not rise because of these initiatives.

Morrisey said, quote, "Power bills will not go up" End quote. And he said he would, quote, "look to enforce that any way I can" End quote.

Emmett Pepper is with Energy Efficient West Virginia, a consumer group focused on electric bills. He told West Virginia Watch he wants to see more details. He pointed to FirstEnergy's gas plant proposal. Quote, "It's not good for the rate payers" End quote.

## p3

My read: the word doing the most work in that pledge is, quote, "existing." End quote.

A promise that existing customers won't carry the cost leaves open who will. And the release doesn't say. It doesn't name a customer for three gigawatts. And it doesn't mention the Public Service Commission.

W-V-P-B ties the governor's wider goal, fifty gigawatts by twenty fifty, to drawing industries like A-I and advanced manufacturing. None of the four accounts I read quotes anyone from Appalachian Power.

I'll give the governor this much. He put the promise in writing. And a written promise can be checked. I think the place to check it is the filings that come later. When the plants have names, and the costs have line items.

On Thursday, Nscale filed an amended S-one. It adds preliminary, unaudited estimates for the nine months through September. A net loss of one billion, six hundred ninety-one point nine million dollars. On revenue of three hundred seven point five million.

For the six months through June, it reports a net loss of one billion, twenty point one million dollars. On revenue of one hundred forty point six million.

By my arithmetic from those two pairs, the third quarter alone came to a loss of about six hundred seventy-two million dollars. On about one hundred sixty-seven million in revenue.

The filing says its auditor, K-P-M-G, has not reviewed the estimates. And the price range is still blank.

## p5

For Mason County, two other lines matter more.

As of this filing, Nscale says it has no binding commitments for any of the financing needed to carry out its Anthropic agreements at Monarch.

And on September twenty-ninth, Anthropic exercised its right to contract for Building Two there. That opened sixty days to negotiate an agreement in good faith. The sixty days are subject to mutual extension. After that, the filing says, neither side has further obligations on Building Two. And Nscale could contract with another party.

By my count, that window runs to about November twenty-eighth. The filing also notes that construction at Monarch has occasionally been halted by flooding.
