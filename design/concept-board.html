The Watch Desk.

A night-shift newsroom for West Virginia's AI buildout. It leads with what is happening and when you can show up, makes the scale physical, and puts a receipt on every claim. Every number on this board comes from the verified research; nothing here is wired to the live site yet.

  1. Lead with what's live. The first screen answers "what's happening this week, and where can I be?"
  2. One spine: place × time. A map with a time scrubber ties projects, hearings, permits, and protests together.
  3. Everything is a case file. One link per project, with its timeline, permits, cases, people, and dates.
  4. Make the scale physical. 864 engines is abstract. 864 dots is not.
  5. Follow the connections. People, firms, projects, and cases, with a source on every line.
  6. Receipts are a feature. Verification status rides on every item. ⚠ Verify is honest and visible.
  7. Built for showing up. Countdown urgency, .ics on every date, a printable sheet per county.

On a phone, the desk becomes a pocket briefing.

  • Countdown chips scroll sideways. The most urgent one is always first and turns red inside 48 hours.
  • "This week near you" remembers one county (stored only on the device) and lists its standing meetings and nearby hearings with addresses.
  • Bottom tab bar with five destinations: Front, Map, Dates, Docket, My county.
  • Every row is a deep link. Tap a date to add it to the phone's calendar; tap a project to open its case file.
  • Unknown dates say so. "Date TBD" rows stay on the list so a pending decision never drops out of sight.

Why a printable sheet?

  • Commission meetings are where residents can still speak. A one-pager travels better than a link.
  • Generated from the same data as the site. Change a record once, and the case file, calendar, and sheet all update.
  • Print CSS flips to the newsprint theme, drops the nav, and adds the QR code. No PDF tooling needed.
  • One sheet for each of the 17 counties with standing meetings in the calendar.
